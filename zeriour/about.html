Our story

From one van in Motijheel to a national network.

Our first route was six merchants in Motijheel and a single van. The promise was narrow: pick up before 4 PM, scan every handover, and call the merchant the moment something went wrong. That promise is still the operating model.

Today the network runs scheduled line-haul between eight hubs, with upazila routes covering all 64 districts. Same-day metro runs in Dhaka and Chattogram; remote districts are served on planned weekly routes rather than optimistic ones.

We deliberately keep COD visible. Merchants can see what was collected per parcel, what fees were deducted and when the payout lands. Reconciliation shouldn’t require a spreadsheet archaeology project.

Every figure on this site belongs to a locally seeded demo dataset. Nothing here connects to a real courier network.
Operating principles

How we make delivery boring.

Reliability is an operating discipline, not a marketing adjective.

Scan every handover

If a parcel changes hands, it is scanned. The timeline is the source of truth for merchants, riders and support.

Make money visible

COD collection, fees and payouts are itemised per parcel. No unexplained deductions.

Promise honestly

Remote districts get realistic transit windows. We would rather quote five days than miss two.

Pay riders fairly

Rider earnings are transparent and tied to completed tasks, not opaque incentives.
